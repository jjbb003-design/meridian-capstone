## Introduction — optional reflection

**Question:** How can I challenge a client's assumptions respectfully when the client already favors a particular answer? In Meridian's case, I want to understand why Dana favors Pasadena without treating that preference as the conclusion of our analysis.

**Quote:** “Reject, embrace, or bend the rules.” — Gorick Ng, introduction, [public preview](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-unspoken-rules/9781647820459/xhtml/introduction.xhtml).

**Comment:** I would distinguish flexible team habits from explicit obligations. I can ask Dana whether a different analysis would better support the location decision, while still following the absolute restriction on putting customer or employee records into AI.

## Chapter 1 — The Three Cs

**Question:** How can I show competence, commitment, and compatibility in the first Meridian interview when I do not yet have the company's data? I want to be prepared and collaborative without pretending that I already know which location is best.

**Quote:** “Can you do the job well?” — Gorick Ng, Chapter 1, [public preview](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-unspoken-rules/9781647820459/xhtml/chapter_1.xhtml).

**Comment:** I would show competence by checking research claims, commitment by arriving with focused questions, and compatibility by listening to Dana's priorities. Being agreeable should not mean accepting Pasadena without comparing evidence and alternatives.

## Chapter 2 — Think, “Let's Give This a Shot!”

**Question:** How do I decide which unfamiliar tasks to try myself and when to ask for help? For the dashboard tutorial, I want to learn by attempting a manageable step while recognizing when an account or data problem needs someone else's input.

**Quote:** “Opportunities will come your way all the time.” — Gorick Ng, Chapter 2, [public preview](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-unspoken-rules/9781647820459/xhtml/chapter_2.xhtml).

**Comment:** I would start by running the tutorial dashboard and checking a displayed total against the source data. Completing and understanding a small step would help me take on more responsibility without promising something I cannot yet explain.
